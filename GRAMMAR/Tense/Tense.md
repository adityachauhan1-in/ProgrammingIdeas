# English Tenses — Complete Notes
## 🧠 Quick Overview

English has **3 time frames**: Past, Present, Future
Each time frame has **4 aspects**: Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous

That gives us **12 tenses** total.

| Aspect \ Time | Past | Present | Future |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple | wrote | write/writes | will write |
| Continuous | was/were writing | am/is/are writing | will be writing |
| Perfect | had written | have/has written | will have written |
| Perfect Continuous | had been writing | have/has been writing | will have been writing |

---

## 1️⃣ PRESENT TENSES

### 1.1 Simple Present
**Structure:** Subject + V1 (add "s/es" for he/she/it)

**Use for:**
- Habits, routines
- General truths/facts
- Fixed schedules

**Examples:**
- She **plays** cricket every Sunday.
- The sun **rises** in the east.
- The train **leaves** at 6 PM.

---

### 1.2 Present Continuous (Present Progressive)
**Structure:** Subject + am/is/are + V-ing

**Use for:**
- Action happening right now
- Temporary situations
- Future plans (already arranged)

**Examples:**
- I **am writing** notes right now.
- She **is studying** for her exam this week.
- We **are meeting** them tomorrow.

---

### 1.3 Present Perfect
**Structure:** Subject + have/has + V3 (past participle)

**Use for:**
- Action completed at an unspecified time in the past, but relevant now
- Experience
- Action that started in the past and continues (with "for/since")

**Examples:**
- I **have finished** my homework.
- She **has visited** Paris twice.
- They **have lived** here since 2015.

---

### 1.4 Present Perfect Continuous
**Structure:** Subject + have/has + been + V-ing

**Use for:**
- Action that started in the past and is still continuing
- Emphasis on duration

**Examples:**
- I **have been working** here for 3 years.
- She **has been studying** since morning.

---

## 2️⃣ PAST TENSES

### 2.1 Simple Past
**Structure:** Subject + V2 (past form)

**Use for:**
- Completed action in the past (specific time)

**Examples:**
- He **watched** a movie last night.
- I **went** to school yesterday.

---

### 2.2 Past Continuous
**Structure:** Subject + was/were + V-ing

**Use for:**
- Action in progress at a specific time in the past
- Two actions happening together in the past
- Interrupted action

**Examples:**
- I **was reading** when he called.
- They **were playing** football at 5 PM yesterday.

---

### 2.3 Past Perfect
**Structure:** Subject + had + V3

**Use for:**
- Action completed before another past action ("past of the past")

**Examples:**
- She **had left** before I arrived.
- I **had finished** my work when he came.

---

### 2.4 Past Perfect Continuous
**Structure:** Subject + had + been + V-ing

**Use for:**
- Action that was ongoing before another past action
- Emphasis on duration before the past event

**Examples:**
- He **had been working** for 5 hours before he took a break.
- They **had been waiting** for an hour when the bus arrived.

---

## 3️⃣ FUTURE TENSES

### 3.1 Simple Future
**Structure:** Subject + will + V1

**Use for:**
- Predictions
- Spontaneous decisions
- Promises

**Examples:**
- I **will call** you tomorrow.
- It **will rain** later today.

---

### 3.2 Future Continuous
**Structure:** Subject + will be + V-ing

**Use for:**
- Action that will be in progress at a specific future time

**Examples:**
- I **will be traveling** at this time tomorrow.
- She **will be attending** the meeting when you arrive.

---

### 3.3 Future Perfect
**Structure:** Subject + will have + V3

**Use for:**
- Action that will be completed before a specific future time

**Examples:**
- I **will have finished** the project by Monday.
- They **will have left** by the time we get there.

---

### 3.4 Future Perfect Continuous
**Structure:** Subject + will have been + V-ing

**Use for:**
- Action that will continue up until a certain point in the future
- Emphasis on duration

**Examples:**
- By next year, I **will have been working** here for 10 years.
- She **will have been studying** for 6 hours by 9 PM.

## 🔑 Key Tips to Remember

- **Simple** → just the fact/action (no extra detail about duration)
- **Continuous** → action in progress (ongoing, use "-ing")
- **Perfect** → action completed before another point in time (use V3/past participle)
- **Perfect Continuous** → ongoing action with emphasis on **duration**, up to a certain point

### Common Confusions

| Confusion | Difference |
|---|---|
| Simple Past vs Present Perfect | Simple past = specific finished time ("I ate at 2 PM"). Present perfect = unspecified time, still relevant now ("I have eaten"). |
| Past Simple vs Past Perfect | Past perfect happens **before** another past action. "I had eaten before he arrived" (eating happened first). |
| Present Continuous vs Simple Present | Continuous = happening now/temporary. Simple = habit/routine. |
